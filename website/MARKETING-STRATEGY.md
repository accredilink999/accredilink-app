## 1. BUYER PERSONAS

### Persona 1: "Concerned Claire" - Adult Child Decision-Maker

**Demographics:**
- Age: 45-62
- Gender: Predominantly female (approximately 65% of primary carers are women)
- Location: Lives in Denbighshire, Conwy, or Wrexham, or has relocated elsewhere in the UK with a parent still in North Wales
- Occupation: Working full-time, possibly part-time due to caring responsibilities
- Household income: GBP 30,000-55,000
- Digital literacy: Moderate to high; uses smartphone daily, active on Facebook, occasionally LinkedIn

**Situation:**
Claire's parent (typically mother, aged 78-90) has had a recent fall, hospital admission, or noticeable cognitive decline. Claire is trying to juggle work, her own family, and increasing concern about Mum or Dad living alone. She may already be providing informal care (shopping, cleaning, check-ins) and is reaching breaking point.

**Pain Points:**
- Guilt about not being able to do everything herself
- Fear of putting a parent into residential care ("Mum always said she wanted to stay at home")
- Overwhelmed by the number of care agencies and unsure how to assess quality
- Worried about safety between carer visits -- what happens if Mum falls at 2am?
- Confused about funding: Does the council pay? What is a direct payment? What can she afford privately?
- Had a bad experience with a previous agency (carers not turning up, different person every visit)

**Search Behaviour:**
- Searches on mobile, often in the evening after the children are in bed
- Google searches: "home care agency near Rhyl," "domiciliary care Denbighshire," "how to arrange care for elderly parent Wales"
- Reads Google reviews before shortlisting -- looks for mentions of reliability and kindness
- Checks CIW website to verify registration
- Visits agency websites and looks for: services offered, coverage area, team photos, and a phone number prominently displayed
- May ask on local Facebook groups ("Can anyone recommend a care agency in Conwy?")

**Emotional Triggers:**
- Reassurance that her parent will be safe, especially overnight and at weekends
- Evidence that the same familiar carers will attend regularly
- Knowing there is someone to call in an emergency outside standard hours
- Testimonials from families in similar situations
- Feeling that the agency genuinely cares, not just a business

**Decision Factors (ranked):**
1. CIW registration and inspection reports
2. Emergency response capability (this is where Accredilink's on-shift responders are a decisive advantage)
3. Continuity of carers
4. Geographic coverage matching the parent's location
5. Responsiveness -- how quickly the agency returns the first enquiry call
6. Price (important but secondary to trust and safety for most private-pay clients)

**Preferred Channels:**
- Google Search (first point of contact)
- Facebook (community recommendations and the agency's own page)
- Agency website (for deeper research)
- Phone call (to make the final decision -- Claire wants to speak to a real person)

**How to reach Claire:**
- Google Ads targeting "domiciliary care" + local area keywords
- Facebook ads using family-oriented imagery (not stock photos -- real North Wales settings)
- Blog content answering her questions: "How to choose a domiciliary care agency in North Wales," "Understanding direct payments in Wales"
- Ensure the website has a clear, reassuring tone with a visible phone number on every page

### Persona 2: "Independent Ivor" - Elderly Private Client

**Demographics:**
- Age: 77-92
- Gender: Male or female (slightly more female due to life expectancy)
- Location: Living alone in own home in Rhyl, Prestatyn, Colwyn Bay, Llandudno, Wrexham, or surrounding villages
- Marital status: Widowed or spouse in residential care
- Income: State pension plus occupational pension and/or savings; homeowner
- Digital literacy: Low to moderate; may use a tablet (often set up by a grandchild), but primarily relies on phone calls and face-to-face contact

**Situation:**
Ivor has been managing on his own since his wife passed away three years ago. He is fiercely independent but is finding it harder to manage daily tasks -- getting dressed, preparing meals, remembering medications. He may have had a recent health scare (fall, TIA, UTI leading to confusion). A GP, district nurse, or neighbour has suggested he "look into getting a bit of help." He does not want to "go into a home."

**Pain Points:**
- Fear of losing independence and dignity
- Does not want strangers in the house
- Worried about cost -- "I've worked all my life, I don't want to spend the house"
- Confused about what domiciliary care actually involves (thinks it means someone moves in)
- May be resistant to help and needs gentle persuasion from a trusted source (GP, family member)
- Concerned about being seen as "past it" by neighbours or community

**Search Behaviour:**
- Rarely searches online himself -- a family member, GP surgery, or social services will do the initial search
- However, if he does search, it will be simple terms: "home help Wrexham," "carer to help at home"
- More likely to respond to a leaflet at the GP surgery, a recommendation from the district nurse, or a mention in a local newspaper or church newsletter
- Word of mouth from friends at the bowling club, chapel, or Morrisons cafe

**Emotional Triggers:**
- Being treated with respect and dignity -- not patronised
- Reassurance that he is still in control ("You choose what help you want and when")
- Meeting the carer before the service starts
- Welsh language availability (especially in Denbighshire and Conwy where Welsh speakers are significant)
- Knowing that help is available quickly in an emergency

**Decision Factors (ranked):**
1. Personal recommendation from someone he trusts (GP, nurse, friend)
2. Meeting the carer and feeling comfortable
3. Flexibility -- can he have help just with mornings, or just with medication?
4. Welsh language provision
5. Cost transparency -- no hidden charges, clear weekly price
6. The agency is local and established, not a national chain

**Preferred Channels:**
- GP surgery leaflets and posters
- District nurse or community nurse recommendation
- Phone call (Ivor will not fill in an online form)
- Local newspaper advertising (Denbighshire Free Press, North Wales Pioneer, Wrexham Leader)
- Community notice boards in libraries, post offices, and chapels

**How to reach Ivor:**
- Leaflet distribution to GP surgeries in Denbighshire, Conwy, and Wrexham
- Build relationships with district nursing teams at Betsi Cadwaladr University Health Board
- Advertise in local print: Denbighshire Free Press, North Wales Weekly News, Wrexham Leader
- Ensure the phone is answered by a person, not a machine, during business hours
- Offer a free, no-obligation home visit as the first step
- Welsh language materials and Welsh-speaking carers prominently mentioned

### Persona 3: "Professional Priya" - Local Authority Care Coordinator / Social Worker

**Demographics:**
- Age: 30-55
- Role: Social worker, care coordinator, or commissioning officer within Denbighshire County Council, Conwy County Borough Council, or Wrexham County Borough Council adult social services
- Education: Degree in social work (registered with Social Care Wales)
- Works from: County Hall Ruthin (Denbighshire), Bodlondeb Conwy (Conwy), or Crown Buildings Wrexham (Wrexham)
- Digital literacy: High; uses council systems daily, email-dependent

**Situation:**
Priya manages a caseload of 25-40 service users who need domiciliary care packages. She is under enormous pressure: demand is rising, budgets are frozen, and reliable agencies are hard to find. She regularly commissions care packages and needs agencies who can pick up new referrals quickly, cover rural areas, and maintain quality. She has been burned before by agencies who accepted packages and then could not staff them, leaving vulnerable people without care.

**Pain Points:**
- Agency reliability -- packages handed back due to staffing shortages
- Rural coverage gaps (Denbigh, Ruthin, Llangollen, Abergele hinterland, Cerrigydrudion)
- Quality concerns -- she is personally accountable if a safeguarding incident occurs with a commissioned provider
- Communication breakdowns -- agencies that do not return calls or update care plans
- CIW compliance -- she needs to refer only to agencies with satisfactory or good inspection reports
- Waiting lists -- she has service users assessed and approved but no agency available to start

**Search Behaviour:**
- Does not Google agencies -- she works from the council's approved provider list
- However, she will check the CIW register for any new agency she is considering
- Attends North Wales regional provider forums and commissioning meetings
- Receives email approaches from agencies (most are generic and immediately deleted)
- Values face-to-face meetings at her office or at provider networking events
- Will check an agency's website to assess professionalism before agreeing to a meeting

**Emotional Triggers:**
- Relief when an agency says "yes, we can start Monday"
- Confidence that the agency understands Welsh social care legislation (Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014)
- Trust that the agency will flag concerns proactively rather than hide problems
- Respect for the professional relationship -- agencies who communicate as partners, not just vendors

**Decision Factors (ranked):**
1. On the approved provider list for the relevant county council
2. Current CIW registration with satisfactory or above inspection
3. Demonstrated capacity to take on new packages immediately
4. Coverage of rural as well as urban areas
5. Emergency response capability (a significant differentiator -- reduces out-of-hours crisis calls to the council)
6. Staff training levels, especially in dementia, moving and handling, medication management
7. Competitive hourly rate within the council's banding
8. Quality of communication and care plan documentation

**Preferred Channels:**
- Email (formal, professional, concise)
- Face-to-face meetings at council offices
- Provider forums and networking events
- Council procurement portal
- Referral from colleagues

**How to reach Priya:**
- Apply to join the approved provider lists for Denbighshire, Conwy, and Wrexham councils
- Attend North Wales Social Care Provider forums
- Send a targeted introduction email (not a mass mailout) to the commissioning teams at each council
- Emphasise the emergency responder capability as a unique safety net
- Highlight training credentials (pre-hospital emergency care is a genuine differentiator)
- Offer to present at team meetings -- a 15-minute slot showing how the emergency response model works
- Maintain impeccable CIW inspection records and share reports proactively

### Persona 4: "Deadline-Driven Dan" - NHS Hospital Discharge Coordinator

**Demographics:**
- Age: 35-55
- Role: Discharge coordinator, discharge liaison nurse, or ward manager
- Location: Glan Clwyd Hospital (Bodelwyddan), Wrexham Maelor Hospital, or Llandudno General Hospital (Betsi Cadwaladr University Health Board)
- Reports to: Site manager or divisional nurse director
- Digital literacy: High within NHS systems; limited time for external browsing

**Situation:**
Dan is under relentless pressure to free up hospital beds. Delayed transfers of care (DTOC) are a constant headline issue for Betsi Cadwaladr UHB, which has been in special measures / targeted intervention. Every patient waiting for a care package to be arranged before they can go home represents a bed blocked and a patient at risk of hospital-acquired infection, deconditioning, and loss of independence. Dan needs care agencies who can mobilise within 24-48 hours.

**Pain Points:**
- Packages of care that take 2-3 weeks to arrange while patients deteriorate on the ward
- Agencies that say they have capacity but then delay start dates
- Complex patients (dual diagnosis, dementia plus physical frailty, end-of-life) that agencies refuse to accept
- Weekend and evening discharge gaps -- most agencies cannot start packages on a Saturday
- Lack of emergency or interim care cover for the first 24-48 hours after discharge
- Communication failures between agency, patient, family, and ward

**Search Behaviour:**
- Works from the health board's list of approved domiciliary care providers
- Contacts agencies by phone when a bed is needed urgently
- Attends multi-disciplinary team (MDT) meetings where agency representatives may be present
- Rarely visits agency websites but will check CIW if a new provider is proposed
- Values agencies that have a named contact he can call directly, not a generic office number

**Emotional Triggers:**
- Immediate relief when an agency says "we can start tomorrow"
- Confidence that the agency can handle complex needs (catheter care, PEG feeds, palliative care)
- Trust that carers will arrive on time so the patient can be discharged by the agreed slot
- Reassurance that there is emergency backup if a carer calls in sick on discharge day
- Knowing the agency's emergency responders can provide immediate safety cover while the full care package is set up

**Decision Factors (ranked):**
1. Speed of response -- can the agency start within 24-48 hours?
2. On the health board's approved provider list
3. Capacity to handle complex health needs
4. Weekend and evening availability
5. Emergency response capability (Accredilink's on-shift responders make this a standout feature for hospital discharge)
6. Named single point of contact for the discharge team
7. Clear handover documentation and communication protocols
8. CIW compliance and quality track record

**Preferred Channels:**
- Direct phone call to a named contact
- Email with a one-page capability summary attached
- Face-to-face visit to the ward or discharge lounge
- Attendance at DTOC review meetings and MDT meetings
- Health board provider events

**How to reach Dan:**
- Register on the Betsi Cadwaladr UHB domiciliary care provider list
- Offer a dedicated discharge liaison contact within Accredilink (a named person with a direct mobile number)
- Visit discharge teams at Glan Clwyd, Wrexham Maelor, and Llandudno General to introduce the service
- Lead with the emergency responder capability: "We can have a responder with your patient within 2 hours of discharge confirmation while the full care package is mobilised"
- Provide a one-page laminated flyer for the discharge office noticeboard: agency name, services, coverage area, direct contact number, CIW registration number
- Attend Betsi Cadwaladr discharge planning forums and DTOC review meetings
- Build relationships with ward managers and site managers through consistent, reliable performance on initial referrals

## 2. MESSAGING PILLARS

### Core Value Proposition Statement (Homepage)

> **Trusted domiciliary care across Denbighshire, Conwy, and Wrexham -- with emergency responders on every shift, so your loved one is never without support.**

Alternative (shorter, for headers and ads):

> **Home care with built-in emergency response. CIW regulated. Serving North Wales.**

### Messaging Pillar 1: Safety Beyond the Care Visit

**The promise:** Your loved one is protected around the clock, not just during scheduled care calls.

**Why it matters:** Most domiciliary care agencies provide care during visit slots and nothing in between. Accredilink has trained emergency care responders on shift, meaning there is a real person ready to attend if something goes wrong between visits. This is a genuine differentiator that no other local agency can match.

**Copy examples:**

- **Website hero section:** "What happens between care visits? With Accredilink, you never have to wonder. Our emergency care responders are on shift and ready to attend -- day or night."
- **Service page:** "Unlike other agencies, we don't clock off when the care visit ends. Our trained emergency responders provide an additional layer of safety that gives families peace of mind and keeps your loved one out of hospital."
- **Facebook post:** "Most care agencies send a carer for 30 minutes and then leave. We keep emergency responders on shift around the clock. Because emergencies don't wait for the next scheduled visit. That's the Accredilink difference."
- **Google Ad headline:** "Home Care + Emergency Response | On-Shift Responders 24/7"

### Messaging Pillar 2: Local People, Local Knowledge

**The promise:** Your care is delivered by people who know your community, speak your language, and understand life in North Wales.

**Why it matters:** National care chains often struggle with rural Welsh geography, Welsh language needs, and local health service pathways. Accredilink is rooted in Denbighshire, Conwy, and Wrexham. Carers know the difference between getting to a farmhouse outside Llangollen and a flat in Rhyl town centre.

**Copy examples:**

- **Website about page:** "We are not a national franchise. We are a North Wales care agency, born and based here. Our team knows the communities we serve -- from the Clwydian Range to the Conwy Valley, from Wrexham town centre to the villages of the Dee Valley."
- **Service page:** "Need a Welsh-speaking carer? We prioritise recruiting Welsh speakers because we know that for many of our clients, especially those living with dementia, their first language is the language of comfort and home."
- **Facebook post:** "Our carers don't just know the care plan -- they know the shortcut through Denbigh, the pharmacy that opens late in Abergele, and which GP surgery to call in Wrexham. Local knowledge matters when you're caring for someone you love."
- **Leaflet headline:** "North Wales care, by North Wales people."

### Messaging Pillar 3: Professionally Trained, Not Just Compliant

**The promise:** Our carers are trained to a higher standard, including pre-hospital emergency care, because your loved one deserves more than the minimum.

**Why it matters:** Most agencies deliver mandatory training (moving and handling, food hygiene, safeguarding) and stop there. Accredilink runs its own training division delivering both care qualifications and pre-hospital emergency care training. This means carers can recognise and respond to medical emergencies, not just call 999 and wait.

**Copy examples:**

- **Website training page:** "We don't just meet the CIW training requirements -- we exceed them. Every Accredilink carer completes our in-house training programme, which includes pre-hospital emergency care skills. That means they can assess, stabilise, and act in a medical emergency, not just make a phone call."
- **For social workers/commissioners:** "Our dual training model -- combining social care competencies with pre-hospital emergency care -- means fewer ambulance calls, fewer hospital admissions, and better outcomes for service users."
- **LinkedIn post:** "We believe training is the foundation of safe care. That's why we invested in building our own training capability, covering everything from person-centred care planning to emergency first response. It's also why local authorities and health boards trust us with their most complex packages."
- **Google Ad description:** "CIW regulated. Carers trained in emergency care. Not just minimum standards -- exceptional standards."

### Messaging Pillar 4: Responsive and Reliable

**The promise:** When you need care, we answer the phone, we start quickly, and we turn up -- every time.

**Why it matters:** The biggest complaint about domiciliary care agencies, in every CIW report and every family's experience, is unreliability. Missed visits, late arrivals, constantly changing carers, and unanswered phones. Accredilink must own reliability as a core promise and back it with evidence.

**Copy examples:**

- **Website homepage:** "We answer your call. We start your care on time. And we send the same familiar faces, not a different stranger every day. Reliability is not a bonus -- it's the bare minimum, and we deliver it consistently."
- **For hospital discharge teams:** "Capacity confirmed within 4 hours. Care started within 48 hours. A named coordinator for every discharge referral. No handed-back packages."
- **Facebook post:** "Megan's mum has the same two carers every morning. She knows their names, they know how she takes her tea, and they notice when something's not right. That's not luck -- that's how we plan our rotas. Consistency isn't an accident at Accredilink."
- **Review response template:** "Thank you for mentioning [carer's name] -- we'll make sure they see your kind words. Continuity of care is something we work hard to maintain because we know how much it matters."

### Ethical Use of Emotional Triggers

Emotional triggers are powerful in care marketing because the decision to arrange care for a loved one is deeply personal. The following triggers should be used honestly and with restraint:

| Trigger | How to use it | Boundary |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| **Guilt** ("Am I doing enough?") | Normalise the feeling: "Many families feel guilty about needing help. Arranging professional care is not giving up -- it's the most caring thing you can do." | Never amplify guilt to create urgency. Never imply that not choosing your agency puts someone at risk. |
| **Fear** ("What if something happens?") | Acknowledge the fear and offer a concrete solution: "Worried about falls between visits? Our emergency responders are on shift, so help is never far away." | Never use scare tactics or worst-case-scenario imagery. |
| **Relief** ("Someone else can help") | Show the transformation: "Since Accredilink started, I actually sleep at night." (Testimonial format.) | Only use real testimonials. Never fabricate or embellish. |
| **Trust** ("These people are safe") | Demonstrate: CIW registration number on every page, real staff photos, named team members, inspection report links. | Never claim awards or accreditations you do not hold. |
| **Pride** ("Mum is doing well at home") | Celebrate independence: "Mrs. Jones is still in her own kitchen, making Welsh cakes, with just a little help from us." | Always obtain consent before using any client's story, even anonymised. |

### Call Tracking Setup

**Recommended approach: Dynamic Number Insertion (DNI)**

Use a call tracking provider such as CallRail, Infinity, or Mediahawk (Mediahawk is UK-based and popular with healthcare clients). Avoid free solutions that compromise call quality.

**Setup:**
1. Purchase 3 tracking numbers (geographic numbers starting with 01 to match the local area, not 0800 or 03 numbers which feel corporate)
   - One number for the website (dynamic insertion based on traffic source)
   - One number for Google Business Profile
   - One number for print materials (leaflets, newspaper ads)
2. Configure dynamic number insertion on the website so the displayed phone number changes based on traffic source (organic, paid, social, direct)
3. Set up call recording (with appropriate GDPR-compliant announcement)
4. Configure call source attribution to feed into GA4 via the call tracking provider's integration
5. Set up missed call alerts so that every missed call triggers an immediate callback from the team

**Key call metrics to track:**
- Total calls by source (organic, paid, GBP, print)
- Calls answered vs missed
- Average call duration (calls over 60 seconds are likely genuine enquiries)
- Peak call times (optimise staffing to answer during high-volume periods)
- First-time callers vs repeat callers
- Call-to-assessment conversion rate

## 8. PAID ADS STRATEGY (GOOGLE ADS)

### Campaign Structure

**Campaign 1: Core Domiciliary Care (Search)**
- Objective: Lead generation (phone calls and form submissions)
- Keywords: Domiciliary care + location, home care agency + location, care at home + location
- Match type: Phrase match and exact match
- Budget allocation: 40% of total budget
- Targeting: Denbighshire, Conwy, Wrexham (see location targeting below)

**Campaign 2: Specific Services (Search)**
- Objective: Lead generation for specific high-value services
- Ad groups:
  - Dementia care at home
  - Hospital discharge care
  - Night care / overnight care
  - Respite care
  - Palliative / end of life care
- Match type: Phrase match and exact match
- Budget allocation: 30% of total budget

**Campaign 3: Emergency Response (Search)**
- Objective: Awareness and lead generation for the unique differentiator
- Keywords: Emergency care at home, care agency with emergency response, emergency carer
- Match type: Broad match (modified with audience signals) and phrase match
- Budget allocation: 15% of total budget
- Note: This campaign targets a smaller search volume but very high intent and uniquely positions Accredilink

**Campaign 4: Training Services (Search)**
- Objective: Lead generation for the training division
- Keywords: Care training courses North Wales, first aid training Wrexham, pre-hospital emergency care course
- Match type: Phrase match
- Budget allocation: 15% of total budget
- Note: Separate campaign to avoid cannibalising care service budget

### Location Targeting Specifics for Wales

**Target locations (use radius targeting for precision):**
- 15-mile radius around Rhyl, Denbighshire (LL18) -- covers Prestatyn, St Asaph, Denbigh, Abergele

**Language targeting:**
- English and Welsh

**Device targeting:**
- All devices; no bid adjustments initially. Review device performance after 30 days and adjust if mobile or desktop significantly outperforms.

**Ad schedule:**
- Run ads 7 days a week
- Increase bids by 20% during peak enquiry hours (identified from call tracking data; likely 9am-12pm and 6pm-9pm weekdays, 10am-2pm weekends)
